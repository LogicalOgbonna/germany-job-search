## Likely Rejection Reasons (Ranked by Probability)

### 1. Credibility Gap: Frontend Engineer Applying as Fullstack

**This is the biggest issue.** Your base CV clearly positions you as a **Senior Frontend Engineer**. The tailored CV was rewritten as a "Senior Fullstack Engineer" with backend claims that aren't substantiated in your actual experience:

| Claimed in Tailored CV | Actual Base CV |
|---|---|
| "Senior Fullstack Engineer (React / Next.js / **Node.js**)" at Roche | "Frontend Software Engineer (ReactJS)" at Roche |
| "**Staff Fullstack Engineer**" at DESCASIO | "**Staff Frontend Engineer**" at DESCASIO |
| "Architected the **fullstack platform**... with Node.js API services and PostgreSQL on the backend" | "Led **frontend** architecture for a clinical trial data platform" |
| "Designed and implemented **RESTful APIs** and database schemas" at DESCASIO | No backend/API work mentioned at DESCASIO |
| "Server-side data processing pipelines in Node.js" | Not mentioned anywhere |
| "Drizzle ORM with Neon" listed as core competency | No ORM or database tool experience in base CV |
| "LLM-powered knowledge capture tools using Vercel AI SDK and LangChain" | No AI/LLM experience in base CV |

**Why this matters:** eventfirst is an early-stage startup founded by ex-Apple/Airbnb leaders. They will Google you, check your LinkedIn, and potentially cross-reference. If your LinkedIn says "Frontend Engineer" but your CV says "Fullstack Engineer," that's an instant credibility red flag. Even if they didn't check, the backend claims read as surface-level keyword stuffing without the depth a true fullstack engineer would demonstrate.

### 2. No Demonstrable Backend Depth

eventfirst's stack is **Drizzle ORM + Neon (Serverless Postgres) + Vercel**. They need someone who can:
- Design database schemas
- Write migrations
- Build API routes in Next.js
- Work with serverless Postgres

Your CV mentions these tools but only as keywords in competencies or inflated bullet points. There are **zero concrete backend achievements** with measurable outcomes (e.g., "reduced query latency by X%", "designed a schema that handled Y records", "built N API endpoints").

### 3. No Verifiable AI/LLM Experience

The role's core mission is "building tools to capture and structure expert domain knowledge" through "complex prompt chains and agentic workflows." The CV claims experience with:
- Vercel AI SDK, LangChain, Mastra
- Agentic workflows, RAG, embeddings, vector search
- "LLM-powered knowledge capture tools"

**None of this appears in your base CV.** For a role where AI/LLM is the central focus, unsubstantiated claims are worse than honestly saying "I'm deeply curious and learning fast." Experienced reviewers (ex-Apple, ex-Airbnb) would immediately notice this.

### 4. Employment Gap & Thin Experience Timeline

- **Roche:** May 2023 - Present (~2.8 years)
- **DESCASIO:** May 2021 - Apr 2022 (~1 year)
- **Gap:** Apr 2022 - May 2023 (13 months unexplained)

The math doesn't add up. Sep 2019 to Mar 2026 is ~6.5 years, not 8+. And with only 2 listed positions totaling ~3.8 years of employment, the "8+ years" claim is hard to justify. A sharp screener would catch this.

### 5. No Portfolio, GitHub, or Side Projects

eventfirst explicitly values "demonstrated skill and impact over years of experience." For a startup role where you'd be one of the first engineers, they want to see:
- Open source contributions
- Side projects (especially anything AI/LLM related)
- A GitHub profile showing coding activity
- A portfolio demonstrating UX intuition

Your application had none of these. Just a LinkedIn link.

### 6. Salary Expectations May Have Been Misaligned

The job required salary expectations. As a CET-timezone remote role from a Berlin-based startup, they likely have a European salary budget. If your expectations were calibrated to US remote rates (given Roche SF remote), this alone could have filtered you out.

## Lessons for Future Applications

### Do Differently

1. **Be honest about your profile.** Apply as a strong frontend engineer who is actively building fullstack and AI skills, not as someone who already has deep experience in both. Authenticity > keyword matching.
2. **Build verifiable proof.** Before claiming AI/LLM experience, ship a side project. Deploy it. Put it on GitHub. Link to it in your CV. Even a small project (a prompt chain builder, a RAG chatbot) would be more convincing than bullet points.
3. **Address gaps proactively.** The 13-month employment gap and missing early career experience need explanation. Were you freelancing? Learning? Contributing to open source? Add it.
4. **Fix the math.** Either include earlier roles to justify "8+ years" or adjust the claim to match reality (~6 years).
5. **Research the company deeply.** Mention specific things about their product, the event planning industry's pain points, or why the founders' backgrounds matter.
6. **Tailor salary expectations to the market.** Research Berlin/European startup salaries for the role level.

### Keep Doing

1. The CV format and visual design are clean and professional.
2. Good use of metrics where they exist (30-40% bundle reduction, 35% load time improvement).
3. Correct identification of the role's key requirements.
